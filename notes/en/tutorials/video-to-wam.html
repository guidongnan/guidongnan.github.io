From Video Generation to World-Action Models ​

Full text (Chinese): 从视频生成转型 WAMCoverage: June 2025 – August 2026, foundational work marked †. Scale: 11 chapters + 2 appendices, 206 cited papers, all dissected individually in Chapter 11.

About this English page

The tutorial itself is written in Chinese. This page is its English companion: it gives the complete chapter-by-chapter structure, states what each chapter argues, and reproduces the four cross-paper conclusions that the tutorial itself calls the most valuable part. Every derivation, table, diagram and per-paper breakdown lives in the Chinese edition; the paper library is fully browsable in English.

Who this is for ​

This tutorial is written for people who already work on video generation and are considering moving into world-action models. It is deliberately the mirror image of World-Action Models, which looks at WAM from the inside and assumes a robotics reader. Here every section answers one question: this thing I already know how to do — does it still count in WAM, and if not, what replaces it?

What this tutorial argues ​

The spine is five discontinuities (Chapter 3) — the points where a habit from video generation stops working:

  1. Conditioning changes from text to action. Text is a soft, global, one-shot condition; action is a hard, per-step, causally binding one.
  2. Bidirectional attention becomes doubly causal. Causal in time and causal with respect to the action stream.
  3. Offline batch inference becomes a closed-loop time budget. Latency stops being a nice-to-have and becomes a correctness condition.
  4. FVD and VBench give way to action following and task success. The old metrics can rank models in the wrong order.
  5. Open-loop long video becomes closed-loop error accumulation. Drift now feeds back through the policy.

A second organising idea is the four jobs in a WAM stack (Chapter 4): policy, planner, evaluator/simulator, and data engine. Two of those — data engine and evaluator — need no real robot, which is why the tutorial calls them a video-generation researcher's largest immediate advantage.

The four cross-paper conclusions (Chapter 11.0) ​

These are aggregated across 30 papers read end to end. They are reproduced here because they are the most transferable part of the tutorial.

1. "Looks good" ≠ "works", and the two rankings can invert. On the admission ladder, Vista scores better on generation quality (FVD 151.3, CD-FVD 51.6) than Epona (FVD 159.4, CD-FVD 86.1) — yet Epona wins on every action-robustness measure (IEC 0.54 vs 0.33, success 0.28 vs 0.08) and has a trustworthy horizon of 3.2 s against Vista's 1.6 s. Pick your evaluator by FVD and you pick the one you can believe for 1.6 seconds.

Report the statistics honestly

On WorldArena, EWMScore correlates with human satisfaction at r = 0.825 over 14 models — significant. But its correlations with data-synthesis usefulness (r = 0.600) and action planning (r = 0.360) were computed over only 6 models, where two-tailed significance at p < 0.05 requires |r| > 0.81. Neither is significant. The defensible statement is "visual quality correlates strongly with human satisfaction; its correlation with embodied utility cannot be established at this sample size" — not "looking good is unrelated to working".

2. Most of a "highly correlated" evaluator's correlation usually comes from a trivial baseline. WorldGym reports per-task r = 0.78, but a predictor that only outputs the policy mean already reaches r ≈ 0.752, and within-group correlation is only r ≈ 0.465. SC3-Eval reports pooled r = 0.929, while "output the criterion mean and ignore the policy entirely" reaches r ≈ 0.79. RoboWorld's r = 0.989 rests on n = 8 policies. The practical instruction: the first figure in any evaluator paper should be the trivial baseline's correlation.

3. The marginal value of synthetic data is far below intuition, and is negative when diversity is low. DreamGen's 240k synthetic trajectories against 720 real ones (333×) yields 20.55% when trained on neural trajectories alone, versus 17.44% from just 30 real demonstrations. RoboCurate, holding the total at 10K trajectories, gets 12.5% success at 25% task diversity — below the 15.4% pure-real baseline — rising to 17.3% at 50% and 19.7% at 100%; action-level filtering contributes about 3.5 points against diversity's 7.2. Priority order: diversity > filtering quality > generation fidelity.

4. Pretraining matters far more than generating the future at inference time — but its source is usually misattributed. Vid2World† shows FVD 1768.8 from random init against 22.4 after transfer. Fast-WAM loses 8.0 points on RoboTwin when video co-training is removed, while inference-time future generation is worth under 1 point. The sharpest result is VPP's ablation: removing internet-scale manipulation data costs −0.36, while removing the fine-tuning of the video model on the downstream domain costs −1.02. Fine-tuning in-domain is roughly three times more important than the internet-scale prior itself.
