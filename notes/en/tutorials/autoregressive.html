What this tutorial is about ​

A bidirectional video diffusion model must decide the whole clip at once. An autoregressive one commits to a prefix and extends it, which buys four things — unbounded length, streaming output, interactive control, and a natural place to inject actions — and costs one thing that dominates the entire literature: exposure bias and the error accumulation it causes.

The tutorial separates three schools (discrete-token next-token prediction, continuous-token AR without quantisation, and autoregressive diffusion / Diffusion Forcing), then argues that the decisive design choices are not the school but (a) the temporal unit, chunk-wise versus frame-wise, (b) teacher forcing versus self-forcing during training, and (c) how the KV cache is managed once the video runs past the training horizon. Chapter 5 dissects representative systems one at a time; Chapter 8 is the grouped index.
