World-Action Models (WAM) ​

About this English page

The tutorial itself is written in Chinese. This page is its English companion: it gives the complete chapter-by-chapter structure and states what each chapter argues. Every derivation, table, diagram and per-paper breakdown lives in the Chinese edition; the paper library is fully browsable in English.

What this tutorial is about ​

"World action model" became a fashionable label faster than it became a definition, and Chapter 0 opens by saying so. The tutorial's response is an admission contract: a system is a WAM only if a prediction of the future is load-bearing for the action it emits. Systems that generate a future and then ignore it are world models with a policy bolted on, not WAMs.

Everything else follows from a four-axis anatomy, introduced in Chapter 2 and used consistently thereafter:

  • Action coupling — how tightly prediction and action are bound (this is the formalisation the tutorial calls the most important in the text).
  • Prediction substrate Φ — pixels, latents, geometry, or affordances.
  • Deployment regime D — the cost model you are actually operating under.
  • Training objective family — what the loss is asking the model to be good at.

Three method families are then defined by where the prediction stops: Render-and-Decode (all the way to pixels), Latent-Only (stops at the latent), and Video-Generation-Free (no pixel video backbone in the prediction path at all). Chapter 8.1 treats "should we generate pixels?" as the field's central unresolved tension rather than a settled question.
